REPORTING PERIOD: JANUARY 2021 – JUNE 2026

01 // Business Problem & Project Objective

Product returns and refund expenses create operational and margin pressure across quick-commerce fulfilment. Before modifying customer return policies—which risks penalising loyal customers—this investigation evaluates 30,271 order-product records across 10,000 unique orders, 1,000 customers, 100 products, 10 product categories, and 5 operating regions (10 cities) in Turkey from January 2021 – June 2026 to quantify potentially avoidable returns and determine where operational improvements should be prioritised.

02 // Relational Dataset Architecture (5 Tables → 30 Columns)

Merged 5 relational tables totaling 26 source columns on 4 shared join keys (OrderID, CustomerID, ProductID, CategoryID): Order Details (30,271 rows, 10 cols), Orders (10,000 rows, 4 cols), Customers (1,000 rows, 7 cols), Products (100 rows, 3 cols), and Categories (10 rows, 2 cols). Removing 4 repeated join keys left 22 distinct base attributes, which combined with 1 duplicate-validation field (Dupl) and 7 derived analytical fields formed the final 30-column dataset.

03 // 5-Stage Data Cleaning & Preparation Methodology

  • Stage 1 — Relational Consolidation: Joined all 5 tables across 30,271 order-product line items.
  • Stage 2 — Duplicate & Type Validation: Verified composite key (OrderID + ProductID) via Dupl; all 30,271 rows verified unique.
  • Stage 3 — Potentially Avoidable Classification: Assigned binary Avoidable = 1 where IsReturned = 1 AND ReturnReason ≠ 'Customer Changed Mind' (1,125 avoidable vs. 299 changed-mind returns).
  • Stage 4 — Financial Value & Cost Derivation: Calculated net customer spend Price Paid = Unit Price × Quantity × (1 − Discount Rate) (₹26,962.47 avoidable return value) and Cost to Business = Unit Cost × Quantity (₹16.89 mean per returned record vs. 18.18 across all records).
  • Stage 5 — Analytical Banding: Engineered PaidPriceCat (5 equal quintiles ~6,054 rows each), Discount Range (7 bands: 0% to Above 25%), Order Time Band (4 active windows: Morning, Afternoon, Evening, Late Night), and Customer Age Group (5 brackets: Under 25 to 55+).

04 // 14-Metric KPI Hierarchy & Hypothesis Scorecard (H1–H8)

  • Primary KPI: Avoidable Return Rate = 3.72% (1,125 ÷ 30,271).
  • Secondary KPIs: Avoidable Share of Returns = 79.00% (1,125 ÷ 1,424); Avoidable Return Value = ₹26,962.47; Average Cost per Returned Record = ₹16.89.
  • Diagnostic KPIs: Overall Return Rate = 4.70% (1,424 ÷ 30,271) plus 9 subgroup diagnostic rates across category, SKU, region, segment, reason, discount, order time, age group, and price quintile.
  • Hypothesis Testing (8 Formal Hypotheses): 5 Supported (H1 Categories, H2 Return Reasons, H4 Operating Regions, H6 Order Time Bands, H8 Age Groups), 2 Partially Supported due to non-monotonic patterns (H3 Discount Bands, H7 Paid Price Quintiles), and 1 Not Supported (H5 Customer Segments: VIP 3.82%, Premium 3.70%, Standard 3.69% — only 0.13 pp spread).

05 // Key Empirical Findings

  • Freshness & Picking Dominate Avoidable Returns: Across 28 avoidable return reasons, Expired Date (#1: 156 returns; 13.87%) and Wrong Item Sent (#2: 114 returns; 10.13%) represent 24.00% of avoidable returns. All three picking errors combined (Wrong Item 114, Wrong Product 68, Wrong Flavor 67) total 249 returns (22.13%), followed by Packaging Defect (65; 5.78%) and Packaging Damage (48; 4.27%).
  • Category & Timing Hotspots: Household Cleaning (5.51%), Bakery (4.87%), and Beverages (4.65%) record the highest avoidable rates, while Late Night (9 PM–12 AM) peaks at 4.32% avoidable (5.28% overall).
  • Discount, Regional & Correlation Insights: Return rates peak in the 21–25% discount band (5.03% avoidable; 6.24% overall) and in the Mediterranean region (4.50% avoidable: Adana 4.61%, Antalya 4.44%), while Marmara drives 55.73% of avoidable volume (627 returns). Off-diagonal Pearson correlations are negligible (|r| ≤ 0.020), confirming linear discount depth or order hour alone does not establish causation.

06 // Strategic Business Recommendations & Governance

  • Pillar 01 — Pre-Dispatch Expiry & Packaging Audits (High Priority): Implement dark-store expiry verification before dispatch to address Expired Date (13.87%) and audit container/seal integrity in Household Cleaning, Bakery, and Beverages.
  • Pillar 02 — Picking Accuracy & Late-Shift Review (High/Medium Priority): Enforce barcode/SKU checks at packing to target the 249 picking errors (22.13%) and review Late Night (9 PM–12 AM) and Evening dispatch workflows.
  • Pillar 03 — 21–25% Discount SKU Review (Medium Priority): Audit the specific products and stock condition in the 21–25% discount band (5.03% avoidable) and Medium price quintile (4.02%) before altering promotional rules.
  • Pillar 04 — Preserve Uniform Customer Return Policies: Because loyalty segments differ by only 0.13 pp (3.69%–3.82%), do not tighten customer return policies; prioritise operational investigations in Mediterranean and high-volume Marmara while gathering larger sample sizes for Black Sea (258 records; 7 avoidable returns).

07 // Scope & Methodological Disclosures

  • Project & Reporting Period: January 2021 – June 2026 (Dataset OrderDate coverage: January 1, 2021 – June 1, 2026).
  • Dataset Scope: 30,271 consolidated order-product records across 10,000 unique orders, 1,000 customers, 100 SKUs, 10 categories, and 5 regions (10 cities).
  • Dataset Provenance: External live operational provenance of the supplied graduation project dataset is unspecified; all metrics reflect structured analysis of the provided relational workbook.
  • Observational & Inferential Boundaries: Descriptive subgroup comparisons and Pearson correlations (all off-diagonal |r| ≤ 0.020) capture observational patterns and do not establish causation or formal statistical significance.
  • Subgroup Sample-Size Caution: Smaller analytical slices—such as the Black Sea operating region (258 total records; 7 avoidable returns) and narrow discount bands (21–25%: 577 records)—require sample-size caution before operational generalisation.
